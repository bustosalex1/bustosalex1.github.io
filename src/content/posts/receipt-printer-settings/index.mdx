## Context

I recently purchased a used Epson TM-T20III thermal receipt printer to tinker
with, and I was surprised to discover that-- despite the printer only having a
single button-- you can manipulate the printer's settings without having to use
some external app. To do this, the printer prints menus and a user can use the
button to navigate through them and modify settings.

Navigating to a new menu simply results in a new menu "page" being printed. For
example, here's a video of me adjusting the print speed setting.

## Getting to the Point

To answer the question posed in the title: I paid \$24.99 for a pack of 10
phenol-free thermal paper rolls. So, about \$2.50 per roll. At 230' of paper per
roll, we get a cost per millimeter of
$0.00003566\frac{\text{\textdollar}}{\text{mm}}$.

I measured the total length of the paper used in the video above to be 680mm. So
the cost to change the print speed setting is simply:

$$
\begin{align*}
\text{Cost to change settings} &= \text{Length of paper used} \times \text{Cost per length of paper} \\
&= 680\text{ mm} \times 0.00003566 \frac{\$}{\text{mm}} \\
&= \$0.02425
\end{align*}
$$

So, at least for this particular setting and paper, it cost around $0.02.

## Wrap-Up

The point of this post isn't _really_ to figure out how much it costs to change
settings on this specific printer. I was just surprised by this interface (which
I've since learned is standard in most receipt printers) and thought it was
particularly interesting and clever.
